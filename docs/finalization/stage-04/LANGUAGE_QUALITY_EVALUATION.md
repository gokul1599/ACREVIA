### 1. Multilingual Evaluation Methodology

Evaluating agricultural language quality differs sharply from standard machine translation: a grammatically elegant sentence that subtly alters a chemical concentration (e.g., translating *"0.6 g/L"* to *"6.0 g/L"*) is a catastrophic failure.

Language quality is audited against four non-negotiable criteria:
1. **Dosage & Unit Invariance:** Numerical figures and associated volumetric units (`ml/L`, `g/L`, `kg/ha`, `cm`) must be perfectly preserved across English, Telugu, and Hindi.
2. **Technical Meaning Preservation:** Agronomic concepts (e.g., *ETL*, *economic injury level*, *systemic fungicide*) must convey exact scientific meaning.
3. **Safety Warning Prominence:** Mandatory CIBRC label notices must remain prominent and culturally unambiguous in vernacular translations.
4. **Agrarian Vernacular Naturalness:** Phrasing must conform to natural Telangana spoken agricultural idioms rather than sterile literal computer translations.
